🔧 Guide

Make it score players your way

The Justaino Score is my opinion of the current FC 26 meta. You might not share it. Peks Lab lets you re-weight the whole thing to your own reading of the game, right down to which PlayStyles matter at which position, and then ranks your club by that instead.

⚖️ One rule runs through all of this: two scores, never both at once. A single switch decides which one the whole hub speaks - the rankings, the Best XI, the Squad Builder draft and the score on a player card. There's no state where half the tool disagrees with the other half, and my original is always one tap away.

Step one

Finding it

It lives with the score it changes, not in the main menu.

  1. Open the tool and let your club load.
  2. In the Lineup column, tap the 📊 Justaino Score tile.
  3. At the top right of that page, tap 🔧 Peks Lab.

That button gains a glowing ring whenever your own weighting is switched on, so you can never be looking at custom rankings without a signal in view. Closing the page drops you straight back on the rankings, already re-sorted.

The control that matters

The switch

Everything else on the page is inert until this is on My Score.

Active score
Justaino ScoreMy Score

Justaino Score is my numbers, and it's the default. My Score is yours. Switching between them never deletes your tuning - it's a genuine A/B, so you can flip back and forth and compare the same position under both readings.

While the switch sits on Justaino Score, the tuning cards below it are dimmed and their sliders won't move. That's deliberate: there's no state where you can drag something that does nothing. And if you switch to My Score without having changed anything yet, it says so - an untouched custom score is still identical to mine.

Somewhere to start

Presets

Four opinions, ready made. A preset is nothing more than a set of the sliders below, so picking one is identical to dragging them there yourself.

PresetWhat it doesGood for
Justaino baseline Clears every custom value, back to my numbers. Starting over without losing the switch.
Stats purist Ranks mostly on raw stat fit for the position (75% stats), and drops how much a PlayStyle+ is worth. If you think PlayStyles are overrated and pace, physical and shooting decide games.
PlayStyle maxxer Flips it: PlayStyles carry most of the score (72%) and a PlayStyle+ counts for more. If you rate a Finesse Shot+ striker over one with better numbers.
OVR respecter Pulls the result back toward the card's in-game OVR (15%). If you want marquee high-OVR cards ranking near the top. This is how the hub scored before v28.

A preset chip only lights up while your settings match it exactly. Drag any slider afterwards and it quietly deselects, because you're no longer on that preset - you're on your own thing.

The biggest lever

Balance

If you change one thing on this page, change this one.

Balance
75%Stats
25%PlayStyles

Every player's score is built from two halves. Stat fit is how well their six face stats suit the position, and PlayStyle fit is how many of the right PlayStyles they own for it. This slider decides the split. My baseline is an even 50 / 50.

The two always total 100, so there's no invalid setting to get wrong.

1 The same two strikers, judged two ways
Vale has the numbers but few useful PlayStyles: stat fit 95, PlayStyle fit 58.
Olsen is the opposite: stat fit 84, PlayStyle fit 96.
At my 50 / 50:  Vale = 0.5×95 + 0.5×58 = 76.5  ·  Olsen = 0.5×84 + 0.5×96 = 90.0  → Olsen well clear.
At 75 / 25:  Vale = 0.75×95 + 0.25×58 = 85.8  ·  Olsen = 0.75×84 + 0.25×96 = 87.0  → almost level.
At 85 / 15:  Vale = 89.5  ·  Olsen = 85.8  → Vale now leads.

One slider, and your best striker changes. This is why the page shows you who moves as you drag rather than making you guess - see Who this moves.

Fine tuning

The three dials

Each one tells you my baseline underneath it, so you can always see how far you've wandered.

OVR tiebreak baseline 1%

How hard the finished score gets pulled toward the number on the front of the card. I keep this at 1%, which makes it a pure tiebreak: it only separates two players whose fit is otherwise level, and can never lift a marquee card above one that genuinely suits the role better.

Turn it up if you want big-name cards ranking high regardless of how they read statistically. Turn it to 0 to ignore OVR completely.

A PlayStyle+ is worth baseline 3.5×

How many ordinary PlayStyles one PlayStyle+ is worth. At 3.5, a striker with Finesse Shot+ gets three and a half times the credit of one with plain Finesse Shot.

Turn it up if you think the plus versions are where games are won. Turn it down if you'd rather reward a card carrying lots of relevant PlayStyles of any kind.

Full marks needs baseline 5 PS+

The ceiling a card is measured against - how many relevant PlayStyle+ a "perfect" card for the position would have. Everyone's PlayStyle half is scored as a fraction of this.

Turn it up and stacking a sixth relevant plus keeps paying off. Turn it down and a well-built card hits full marks sooner, which compresses the top of your rankings.

Advanced

Stat weights, position by position

Everything from here on lives under Advanced, folded away until you want it.

Pick a position and you get a slider for each of the six face stats. This is where you say what a position actually needs: that your centre backs must be quick, that your CDMs live or die on passing, that shooting barely matters at full back.

Stat weights · CB
PAC 7 SHO 1 PAS 4 DRI 3 DEF 10 PHY 9
  • Only the ratios matter, not the scale. Doubling every number changes nothing at all - what counts is that DEF 10 is worth ten times SHO 1.
  • Skill moves and weak foot ride along as two light extra stats, so you can decide a five-star weak foot is worth something at ST and nothing at CB.
  • Keepers are different. Pick GK and you get the six goalkeeper stats instead, and no skill moves or weak foot rows, because those never applied to keepers.
  • Positions you've edited get a • next to them in the dropdown, and each has its own Reset this position so you can undo one without losing the rest.
Advanced

Your own PlayStyle list

The deepest control on the page, and the one that changes the score most.

Out of the box, each position is scored against a set of roles - an Advanced Forward wants different PlayStyles from a Target Forward - and every card is judged by whichever role it fits best. You can see the whole set on the how the score works page.

If you disagree with those lists, take the position over. You get a weight slider for every PlayStyle, you can add any PlayStyle that isn't there, and you can remove ones you don't rate. It starts seeded with the numbers that position already uses, so you're adjusting my opinion rather than starting from a blank page.

PlayStyle weights · CB
Anticipate 9 Intercept 4 Bruiser 3 Jockey 3 Rapid 5 Block 3
Know what taking over does. A position with your own list stops being scored by role. That's the point: once you've said which PlayStyles matter at CB and by how much, there's nothing left for a role to be fitted against. Anything not on your list is worth nothing at that position. Every other position carries on by role exactly as before, and "Score CB by role again" hands it straight back whenever you want.

Two smaller notes. Keeper-only PlayStyles (Far Reach, Footwork, 1v1 Close Down, Cross Claimer, Deflector) are only offered for GK. And a PlayStyle+ still counts your weight multiplied by the PlayStyle+ dial, so those two settings work together.

Advanced

Role priority curve

How much credit a role's ordered list gives each rank.

Every role lists its PlayStyles in priority order. These four numbers decide what each rank in that list earns. My baseline is 4 / 3 / 2 / 1: the top two PlayStyles score 4, the next two score 3, the next two score 2, and everything else scores 1.

Steepen it

Try 6 / 2 / 1 / 0. Only a role's top couple of PlayStyles really count, and owning the rest earns you nothing. Use this if you think one or two PlayStyles define a role and the rest is noise.

Flatten it

Try 3 / 3 / 2 / 2. Anything a role considers relevant counts for nearly the same. Use this if you'd rather reward a well-rounded card than a specialist.

This has no effect on a position where you've taken over the PlayStyle list, since that position is no longer using roles at all.

Advanced

Squad Builder draft

Why your Gauntlet squads move less than your rankings do.

The Squad Builder doesn't draft purely on the score. It blends in a little raw OVR - 10% by default - so that when two players are genuinely level on merit, the better card gets the shirt. The other 90% is whichever score is active, so your squads follow your weighting.

Turn it up if you'd rather the builder simply started your strongest cards and used the score only to settle close calls. At high values a well-kitted lower-rated player will lose their place to a big name with the wrong PlayStyles - which may be exactly what you want when you're filling several squads at once.

2 Why the number lies a little
This setting is a weighted average of two numbers that aren't on the same scale, and only the spread of a number affects the order things come out in.
Measured on a real 546-player club: OVR averaged 81.6 with a spread of 11.75, while the score averaged 53.8 with a spread of 20.2.
The 28-point gap between those averages does nothing at all - it's a constant, and it cancels out. The score's wider spread is what does the work, which meant a setting once labelled 60% was really behaving like 47%.

So treat this dial as a direction rather than a precise percentage. The reliable test is the one below: build the same formation at two settings and see how many shirts change hands.

Put it together

Worked examples

Four opinions about football, and the settings that express them.

"Pace beats everything"

You think a quick centre back who can't head a ball still beats a slow monster who can.

Advanced → Stat weights → CB
Drag PAC up to 10-12
Drag PHY down to 5
Repeat for RB / LB

Watch: set Who this moves to CB. Your quickest defenders should climb several places.

"Only PlayStyles matter"

You think face stats are noise and the meta is decided by which PlayStyle+ a card carries.

Preset → PlayStyle maxxer
Push Balance further to 15 / 85
Raise A PlayStyle+ is worth to 5-6×
Raise Full marks needs to 7

Why the last one: with a low ceiling, cards with three good pluses all hit full marks together. Raising it keeps rewarding the fourth and fifth.

"My CBs need Anticipate, full stop"

You have a specific read on what wins duels, and my role lists don't match it.

Advanced → PlayStyle weights → CB
Use my own list for CB
Anticipate to 9, Intercept to 6
Remove the ones you don't rate, add Rapid at 5

Remember: CB now scores on this list alone. Anything you removed is worth zero there.

"Just start my best cards"

You're filling three Gauntlet squads and you'd rather the big names played than the perfectly-kitted squad players.

Advanced → Squad Builder draft
Drag Lean on OVR up to 50-70%
Reopen the Squad Builder

By default this sits at 10%, so squads follow your score. Turning it up hands the shirt back to the higher-rated card whenever it's close.

New in v39

How Suggest uses this

Suggest used to read off a fixed list I'd written for each role. It now works out which PlayStyles actually raise your score on this card - so if you've re-weighted a position, Suggest re-weights with you.

The old version had a real gap in it: you could re-rank your entire club by your own weighting, then hit Suggest and get my picks anyway. The two halves of the tool disagreed with each other. They don't now.

Which weights it follows In order
  1. Switch off? My baseline, same as everywhere else in the hub. Your settings are ignored until the switch is on - that's not special to Suggest.
  2. Your own PlayStyle list for that position? Yours wins outright. It replaces the role system entirely, so the role dropdown is ignored and the status line reads "for CDM / your list".
  3. No list for that position? My role lists supply the PlayStyle rankings - but your dials still change the answer, see below.

It's decided per position. Take CDM over and leave ST alone, and CDM follows you while ST follows me.

The dials that move the picks
  • PlayStyle+ multiplier - decides whether upgrading a good PlayStyle the card already has beats adding a new one.
  • Full marks needs - sets the ceiling, so it decides when Suggest stops. Raise it and it keeps finding picks worth making; lower it and it finishes sooner.
  • Balance - scales how much a PlayStyle moves the total. Push the slider hard towards stats and Suggest will offer less, which is the honest answer to "PlayStyles barely matter to me".

What it actually does

The trick is that it never estimates. To price a PlayStyle it builds the card you'd have if you took it, then scores that card by your own rules and sees what the number did. A suggestion isn't a guess about your score - it is your score.

  1. Works out which weights to use - your list, else the role you picked.
  2. Takes stock of the card: what it already holds, and how many PlayStyle+ and basic slots are free.
  3. Prices every option. Every PlayStyle the card could still take, in both forms, scored as the card it would become.
  4. Takes the best one and adds it to the working card.
  5. Goes back to step 2. Prices change, because what a PlayStyle is worth depends on what's already there.
  6. Stops when the slots are full, or when nothing left would move the number.

Here's a bare CDM, built as a Holding midfielder:

Suggest · CDM / Holding
1Intercept · PS++13.7
2Pinged Pass · PS++13.8
3Tiki Taka · PS++10.3
4Bruiser · PS++10.2
5Jockey · basic+1.5
Stopped with 3 basic slots free · score 40.2 → 89.7

The four best go in the four PlayStyle+ slots. The fifth pick is worth +1.5 and the sixth is worth nothing at all, so it stops - with slots to spare.

Now give that same card a basic Tiki Taka to start with. The fourth pick becomes upgrade Tiki Taka to Tiki Taka+ at +7.3, rather than adding another new style - and because an upgrade hands the basic slot back to you, it can spend that too.

Fewer picks than free slots is the right answer, not a bug. The PlayStyle score is measured against a ceiling, so past a point extra PlayStyles are worth literally nothing. Suggest would rather tell you the score is already maxed than spend your evos on decoration. If you want it to keep going, that's the Full marks needs dial.
It builds for the role you picked, not whichever role flatters the card. The Rankings page is free to score a card as whatever role suits it best - Suggest isn't, because you asked a specific question. That's why changing the role genuinely changes the answer, and why the status line quotes the score as that role.

One last thing worth saying plainly. Suggest and the score now share a single piece of maths, so they cannot drift apart. Which means a suggestion you disagree with is a weighting you disagree with - and this page is where you go and win that argument.

Before you go deep

Things to watch

None of these will break anything. They're the places where the maths behaves in a way you might not expect.

A very short PlayStyle list makes scores swingy. Your list also sets the ceiling that the 0-100 score is measured against. Strip a position down to three PlayStyles and any card owning them will score enormously, while everyone else collapses. That's why taking a position over seeds you with the full list rather than an empty one.
Settings live in this browser only. They're not stored on your EA account, so they don't follow you to another device, and clearing site data clears them. Nothing in the game is ever touched by changing them.
If the browser's storage is full, saving fails. The EA web app fills it with its own console history. The page will tell you plainly when this happens, and how to free space up. Until you do, your changes work but won't survive a reload.
The score is a judgement, not a fact. Mine included. It reads a card's stats and PlayStyles against an opinion about the meta - it can't know that a card feels wrong on the pitch. Use it to sort a big club quickly, not to overrule what you've seen in a game.
Loose ends

Questions

Can I lose my tuning by switching back to the Justaino Score?

No. The switch only changes which score is active. Your values sit safely underneath and come straight back when you switch again. The only thing that erases them is Reset to Justaino, which asks first.

Do I have to press Save?

There's no Save button. Everything applies and saves the moment you move it, which is what makes the live re-ranking possible. Closing the page just takes you back to your rankings.

Does any of this change my club or my players?

No. Peks Lab only changes how the tool ranks what you already own. It never applies an evolution, never edits a card, and never touches your active squad. The one thing it influences that writes to your account is the contents of a squad you explicitly choose to create from the Best XI or Squad Builder page.

Do my Gauntlet squads follow my weighting too?

Yes. The Squad Builder drafts on whichever score is active, with only a light 10% pull toward raw OVR to settle genuine near-ties. If you'd rather it simply started your highest-rated cards, turn Advanced → Squad Builder draft → Lean on OVR up.

One difference worth knowing: the Best XI page ranks on the score alone, with no OVR pull at all, so the two can still disagree slightly at the margins.

I customised one position. What happens to the others?

Nothing. Every setting is stored per position, so tuning CB leaves ST exactly as it was. The same is true of PlayStyle lists: taking CB over doesn't affect how any other position is scored.

What does the • next to a position mean?

You've edited that position. It's there so you can tell at a glance which ones you've changed and which are still on my numbers, without opening each one.

If the Justaino Score gets retuned for a new season, do my settings break?

No, and this was deliberate. Only the values you actually changed are stored. Anything you left alone follows my updated numbers automatically, so you inherit a seasonal retune for everything except the parts you had an opinion about.
